# Paper 3 — two-system design addendum

**Date:** 15 Oct 2026 · **For:** Dr. Forrest · **Status:** design decision to fold into Protocol v0.2
**Decision:** port the **capacity framework** (not policy, not capital) to Canada, and make cross-system comparability a **tested hypothesis** rather than a working assumption.

---

## 1. What was decided, and why this framework and not the others

Of the programme's three systems (policy / workforce / investment), the **workforce leg** is the one where a Canadian arm is both legitimate and load-bearing:

- **Policy (Paper 2b):** a comparator there reads as ranking systems — correctly rejected for a Canadian audience.
- **Capital (Paper 4):** a Canadian arm dilutes the digital-neocolonial critique the paper is built on (deck slide 13). Wrong entity.
- **Capacity (Paper 3):** comparison runs through a **shared instrument under test**, not through a verdict on a system. Neither system is the benchmark; the template is. That is a structural reason for neutrality, not a rhetorical one.

So Paper 3 acquires a second system — and the design below is what makes that a contribution rather than a convenience.

---

## 2. The methodological core: comparability as hypothesis, not premise

**Dr. Forrest's framing:** *"with an assumption that competencies are similar, then testing that hypothesis."*

This is the technically correct requirement, and it is what most cross-system curriculum studies skip. **IMIA 2023 and AMIA 2017 are Northern-hemisphere frameworks.** Coding African and Canadian programmes with them and reporting "coverage" presumes the *domains are the right units of analysis in both settings*. Stated as an assumption, that is a vulnerability. Stated as a hypothesis, it is the paper's method.

The construct has an established name in measurement: **invariance**. You cannot compare groups on a scale until you establish the scale behaves comparably in each group. Paper 3 should say so explicitly and do it before the comparison.

### What "testing it" means operationally

1. **Structural:** does the same domain structure emerge in both sets, or do programmes in one system load onto domains the other lacks?
2. **Coder-level:** report **Cohen's κ per system**, not pooled. Where does disagreement concentrate, and is it systematic by system?
3. **Profile:** do programmes cluster into comparable domain profiles, or does one system weight domains the other deprioritises?
4. **Unique domains:** identify domains present in one system only. Plausible asymmetries: African programmes may carry community-health-worker data systems, mHealth-at-scale, and disease-surveillance informatics; Canadian programmes may carry privacy-law/procurement specialisations. **Those are findings, not coding failures.**

## 3. What this buys the paper

The headline shifts from a *finding* to a *test the finding depends on*:

- **Before:** "African health-informatics programmes cover X% of responsible-AI competencies."
- **After:** "The competency framework used across the informatics literature behaves comparably in two systems — and *then* the coverage gap is characterised."

The **Layer-B responsible-AI overlay** is the highest-risk artefact in the protocol (§8 of v0.1 calls it "the item the paper stands or falls on"). Under a two-system design it gets something it currently lacks: **an external validity check.** If responsible-AI domains (bias/fairness, validation/monitoring, procurement economics) surface in *both* systems' curricula, the overlay is not a construct invented from your policy findings — it is a construct that is *recognisable in independent training programmes*. That converts Layer B from assertion into measurement.

**Consequence for ordering:** the addition does not postpone the African census. The Canadian set is the **pilot and calibration sample** the protocol already wanted (§8 step 4: pilot coding before full extraction). What changes is that the pilot is *published as part of the design* rather than discarded.

## 5. Fit to venue — why this is also the right audience move

The invariance design suits the target venues better than the original single-system census did:

- **HELINA / AMIA / MedInfo / JMIR** are methods-forward. "A competency framework's portability, tested across two systems" is a **methods contribution** with a substantive application — a stronger abstract than another national coverage census.
- It answers the reviewer question the original protocol could only deflect: *"how do you know your template applies here?"*
- It gives **Thumath (UBC Nursing)** real analytical work (Canadian frame design, invariance interpretation), not a review role.

## 6. What must not drift

- **Do not let the Canadian arm become the story.** It is the *test instrument's portability*; the African census remains the paper's substantive object. Say this in the abstract or the paper reads as a Canadian study with African data.
- **Do not pool the systems for a combined coverage figure.** Pooling presumes the invariance you are testing. Report separately first, always.
- **Do not claim the systems are comparable before the invariance step.** The whole point is that comparability is the finding.
